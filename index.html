// Layer 04

How They Learn

Human Brain
Biological Learning
  • Hebbian plasticity: "fire together, wire together" — synaptic strength increases with co-activation
  • Hippocampal consolidation during sleep converts short-term to long-term memory
  • One-shot learning: a single frightening event can form a lifelong memory
  • Dopaminergic reward signals reinforce beneficial behaviors automatically
  • Continuous learning throughout life without forgetting prior knowledge
LLM / Neural Net
Computational Learning
  • Gradient descent: compute error, backpropagate derivatives, nudge all weights slightly
  • Requires millions-to-billions of examples to generalize robustly
  • Catastrophic forgetting: learning new tasks can overwrite prior skills unless managed
  • RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) mimics reward-based learning
  • Discrete training phases; frozen weights during inference (no real-time learning)
Shared Principles
Where They Converge
  • Both use error-driven adjustment to improve over time
  • Both form distributed, not localized, representations
  • Both exhibit generalization to unseen scenarios
  • Both benefit from structured, varied exposure during training/development
  • Both show emergent capabilities not explicitly programmed
  • Transfer learning mirrors how humans apply knowledge across domains

Where They Diverge

🔋
Brain
~20 Watts
The entire human brain runs on roughly 20 watts — less than a dim light bulb. Extraordinarily energy-efficient due to sparse, event-driven spiking.
⚡
LLM
Megawatts (Training)
Training a frontier LLM consumes millions of kilowatt-hours. Even inference at scale requires dedicated data centers with massive power draws.
🌍
Brain
Embodied & Grounded
Human cognition is deeply tied to a physical body — proprioception, emotion, pain, hunger all shape reasoning in fundamental ways. Thinking happens in a body.
💻
LLM
Disembodied Text
LLMs have no sensory experience of the world. All knowledge comes from text patterns. There is no hunger, fear, or physical intuition grounding the model.
💡
Brain
One-shot Learning
A human can learn a new concept from a single vivid example, especially when emotionally charged. The hippocampus binds features rapidly into memory.
📦
LLM
Massive Data Hungry
Current LLMs need trillions of tokens of training data to generalize. Few-shot capabilities exist but true one-shot learning at human level remains unsolved.
🎭
Brain
Consciousness & Qualia
Humans have subjective experience — the "redness" of red, the ache of sadness. These qualia are deeply real and shape all cognition and motivation.
🔣
LLM
No Inner Experience
LLMs produce language that describes emotion and experience, but there is no evidence of inner subjective states. It's sophisticated pattern matching, not feeling.

// Layer 07 · History

Brain-Inspired AI
1958 → Today

Every major AI breakthrough borrowed something from the brain. Here's how the two fields co-evolved over six decades.

🧠
1958
Perceptron
Frank Rosenblatt at Cornell builds the first trainable artificial neuron, inspired directly by McCulloch-Pitts neuron model of 1943. Learns to classify images using weighted inputs — the first hardware AI.
Brain → AI
❄️
1969
First AI Winter
Minsky & Papert's "Perceptrons" book exposes XOR limitation. Funding dries up. The brain analogy seemed too simplistic — researchers abandon neural approaches for symbolic AI for a decade.
AI Setback
⚡
1986
Backpropagation
Rumelhart, Hinton & Williams publish backprop, enabling multi-layer networks to learn. Directly modelled on synaptic error correction signals hypothesised in biological neural systems.
Brain-Inspired
👁️
1989
Convolutional Networks
LeCun's LeNet applies CNNs to digit recognition — directly modelled on Hubel & Wiesel's 1959 discovery of hierarchical edge detectors in the visual cortex. V1 → V2 → IT cortex becomes Conv → Pool → FC.
Visual Cortex → CNN
💾
1997
Long Short-Term Memory
Hochreiter & Schmidhuber's LSTM introduces gated memory cells — inspired by how hippocampal circuits selectively consolidate and forget information over time. Solves vanishing gradient by mimicking biological memory gating.
Hippocampus → LSTM
🚀
2012
Deep Learning Revolution
AlexNet wins ImageNet by a 10% margin using deep CNNs on GPUs. Hinton, LeCun & Bengio's decades of brain-inspired work suddenly scales. The ventral visual stream is now computationally reproducible for the first time.
Deep Learning Era
🎲
2014
Dropout Regularisation
Srivastava & Hinton introduce dropout — randomly silencing neurons during training. Later linked to cognitive reserve: brains with more redundant connections tolerate more neurodegeneration before symptoms appear.
AI → Neuroscience
🎯
2017
Transformer & Attention
"Attention Is All You Need" (Vaswani et al., Google Brain) replaces recurrence with self-attention — mirroring how the prefrontal cortex dynamically weights sensory inputs. Every modern LLM is built on this architecture.
PFC Attention → Transformer
🔗
2017
EWC — Catastrophic Forgetting Solved
DeepMind's Elastic Weight Consolidation paper demonstrates AI can learn sequentially without forgetting — by protecting important weights, mimicking synaptic consolidation. Directly bridges AI to Alzheimer's memory research.
Synapse → EWC
🌐
2020
GPT-3 & Large Language Models
175B parameter models show emergent abilities — reasoning, analogy, few-shot learning — not explicitly trained. Mirrors how cortical column formation gives rise to abstract thought beyond the sum of individual neurons.
Emergent Cognition
🧬
2021
AlphaFold2 — Protein Revolution
Neural networks predict protein folding with atomic accuracy — cracking a 50-year biology problem. Opens direct path to drug discovery for tau/amyloid proteins causing Alzheimer's and Parkinson's. 200M+ structures released.
AI → Medicine
✨
Now
Neuromorphic AI + BCI
Intel Loihi 2, IBM NorthPole, and BrainScaleS chips run spiking neural networks at biological efficiency. Brain-computer interfaces (Neuralink, BrainGate) begin restoring motor function in ALS patients. The loop closes — AI helping repair the brain.
Full Circle

✓ Where the Analogy Actively Helps Research

01
Alzheimer's Progression Modelling via GNN
Graph neural networks map how connectivity loss spreads across the brain — mirroring how weight collapse propagates through network layers. A GNN-based model using rs-fMRI data achieved 82.9% accuracy predicting transition to more severe cognitive impairment stages.
📄 Moghaddami et al. · arXiv 2604.06469
02
AI Speech as Early Alzheimer's Diagnosis
NIA-funded research using LLMs to analyze speech transcripts predicted progression from mild cognitive impairment to Alzheimer's with over 78% accuracy. Acoustic + linguistic feature integration now achieves above 85% in benchmark datasets (ADReSS / ADReSSo).
📄 Amini et al. · NIA / Alzheimer's & Dementia 2024
03
Catastrophic Forgetting = Memory Model
DeepMind's landmark EWC paper showed that task-specific synaptic consolidation in AI directly mirrors how brains protect memories — and how Alzheimer's erases recent memories first. The algorithm was inspired by synaptic consolidation in mammalian brains.
📄 Kirkpatrick et al. · PNAS 2017 · DeepMind
04
AlphaFold → Protein Drug Discovery
DeepMind's AlphaFold2, released 2021, predicted 200M+ protein structures including tau and amyloid implicated in Alzheimer's and Parkinson's. AlphaFold3 (2024) extends this to protein complexes, directly accelerating drug target identification for neurodegenerative disease.
📄 Thornton et al. · Curr. Opinion Struct. Biology 2023
05
Dropout → Cognitive Reserve Insight
AI dropout — randomly silencing neurons to build robustness — directly parallels how cognitive reserve protects against neurodegeneration. Brains with higher synaptic redundancy tolerate more damage before symptoms appear, mirroring dropout's ensemble effect.
📄 Stern et al. · Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews 2017
06
Neuromorphic Chips for Neural Repair
Intel's Loihi 2 (2024 Hala Point system: 1M digital neurons, 120M synapses per chip) and IBM's NorthPole neuromorphic chips mimic spiking neuron dynamics. These are driving brain-computer interface research for ALS, spinal injury, and neural prosthetics.
📄 Intel Hala Point · HPCwire / Intel Labs 2024

✗ Where the Analogy Breaks Down for Treatment

🧬
No Protein Biology
LLMs don't accumulate tau tangles or amyloid plaques — the physical proteins that physically kill neurons. No computational mirror exists for plaque clearance.
🩸
No Vascular System
Blood-brain barrier breakdown is central to many diseases. LLMs have no circulatory analog — so vascular dementia has no direct AI model.
🛡️
No Glial Cells
Astrocytes and microglia — the brain's immune and support cells — play a massive role in neurodegeneration. AI has no equivalent support architecture.
⏳
LLMs Don't Age
Oxidative stress, mitochondrial decay, and telomere shortening have no AI analog. Age-related neurodegeneration cannot be simulated by weight degradation alone.
⚗️
The Analogy as a Thinking Tool
The Brain↔LLM analogy is most powerful as a mathematical language for neuroscience — letting researchers describe, simulate and predict network failure. But treating actual disease requires going beyond the analogy into biology, chemistry and medicine that no LLM captures. The two fields are strongest together: AI as the model, biology as the ground truth.
